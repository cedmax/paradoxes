setup: "A vanishing puzzle is a mechanical illusion made of several pieces. Arranged one way, the picture shows a certain number of objects. Arranged another way, it shows a different number. Wikipedia reports that Wemple and Company sold a card called The Magic Egg Puzzle in New York in 1880. Cutting the card into four oblongs let the pieces be rearranged to show 8, 9 or 10 eggs. In 1896 the chess player and puzzle maker Sam Loyd patented rotating versions on a pinned disc. In The Disappearing Bicyclist, pointing the arrow at A shows 13 boys, and pointing it at B shows 12."
whyWrong: "Wikipedia lists the [missing square puzzle](missing-square-puzzle) and the [chessboard paradox](chessboard-paradox) as similar puzzles. In the missing square puzzle the viewer assumes that rearranged pieces still fill exactly the same space, and they do not: one arrangement hides a thin sliver of extra area along a bent edge. The vanishing puzzles are presented as the same kind of trick, with pictures cut into pieces."

  - "Wikipedia’s article on vanishing puzzles describes the history and lists the missing square puzzle and the chessboard paradox as similar puzzles. It does not give a worked explanation of any one picture. The explanation here is the one given for the missing square puzzle, where one arrangement hides a thin sliver of extra area."
  - "Wikipedia notes that Loyd offered prizes of $5 to $100 for the best explanation of one of these illusions. The names of the winners were published, but their explanations were not."
