problem: "What happens to the information about what fell into a black hole once the black hole has evaporated?"
claimA: "In ordinary quantum theory, the information about a system’s starting state is never lost as it changes."
claimB: "A black hole that evaporates leaves only featureless heat radiation, which cannot carry information about what fell in."
whatBreaks: "The contested choice is between giving up the rule that quantum physics never loses information, and giving up part of Hawking’s calculation of how a black hole radiates."
statusNote: "the predominant view is that information is preserved and Hawking’s calculation must be corrected. The rivals are that information is lost, that a leftover remnant stores it, and that a firewall changes what falling in is like."
setup: "Throw something into a black hole. Hawking’s calculation says the hole slowly radiates and can eventually disappear. The radiation appears to be heat with no record of what fell in. Ordinary quantum evolution, however, preserves information about the starting state."
whyWrong: "If the hole disappears and its radiation has no such record, information is lost. But preserving information is part of the ordinary quantum rules. The difficulty is making the evaporation calculation and those rules fit together."
resolution:
  - "One route keeps information preservation and changes the evaporation account. Another allows information to be lost; others leave a remnant or change what happens at the boundary. The positions below set out their costs."
positions:
  - title: "Information escapes"
    body: "Information preservation is the predominant view. The radiation would contain subtle correlations that preserve the record, and calculations in certain models support this. Cost: there is disagreement over how to correct Hawking’s calculation and give the full physical account."
  - title: "Information is lost"
    body: "Let gravity break the ordinary rule that information is preserved. Cost: this changes a basic rule of quantum evolution."
  - title: "Something remains"
    body: "A remnant survives and stores the information. Cost: explaining how evaporation stops and how the remnant holds the record is difficult."
  - title: "The boundary is different"
    body: "A firewall proposal changes the smooth picture of falling through the boundary. Cost: it conflicts with the expectation that crossing it need not involve anything locally special."
finePrint: "Hawking’s calculation dates from the 1970s. Preserving information here means reversible quantum evolution, called unitarity, not simply keeping a readable message. Gravity–quantum duality proposals and calculations of radiation entropy in certain models support preservation. They do not by themselves identify every physical step in evaporation. A firewall is a response to a further clash among assumptions about the boundary and quantum correlations, not a complete standalone account of evaporation. Other proposals include fuzzballs, soft hair and baby universes."
