problem: "What happens to the information about what fell into a black hole once the black hole has evaporated?"
claimA: "In ordinary quantum theory, the information about a system’s starting state is never lost as it changes."
claimB: "A black hole that evaporates leaves only featureless heat radiation, which cannot carry information about what fell in."
verdict: "Most physicists now believe the information survives, but how Hawking’s calculation should be corrected is not agreed."
whatBreaks: "The contested choice is between giving up the rule that quantum physics never loses information, and giving up part of Hawking’s calculation of how a black hole radiates."
statusNote: "experts agree the puzzle is real and disagree on which claim to give up. Most physicists now believe information is preserved, but the paradox is still described as an unsolved problem in physics, and views differ on how to correct Hawking’s calculation."
setup: "In 1973 to 1975 Stephen Hawking showed that black holes should radiate. He argued that the radiation depends only on the black hole’s mass, charge and spin, so a black hole that evaporates leaves behind featureless thermal radiation. That would turn a pure starting state, which has all its information, into a mixed one that does not. The rule in quantum physics, called unitarity, says that evolution is reversible, so the information cannot be erased."
whyWrong: "Both claims have strong support. Unitarity is built into quantum mechanics. Hawking’s calculation follows from taking quantum fields in the curved space around a black hole seriously. Something in one of them has to give."
resolution:
  - "Researchers have proposed many ways out. Four of the main ones are below."
positions:
  - title: "Information is preserved"
    body: "Since the 1997 AdS/CFT proposal, a type of duality between gravity and quantum theory, the predominant belief has been that information is preserved. Don Page showed in 1993 that the entropy of the radiation should rise and then fall back to zero if evaporation is unitary. In 2019 researchers amended the calculation of that entropy in certain models. Cost: views differ on how Hawking’s calculation should be corrected."
  - title: "Information is lost"
    body: "The information really is destroyed, as Hawking first argued. This is now a minority view. Penrose is associated with the view that unitarity fails once gravity matters. Cost: it gives up unitarity, the reversibility of quantum evolution."
  - title: "A remnant keeps it"
    body: "Evaporation stops when the black hole reaches the Planck size, leaving a remnant that holds the information. Cost: for large remnants, there is no accepted mechanism to stop the radiation while the black hole is still macroscopic."
  - title: "A firewall at the horizon"
    body: "In 2012 the firewall paradox argued that a wall of high-energy matter at the horizon would avoid violating the monogamy of entanglement. Cost: it conflicts with the equivalence principle, which says an observer falling through a horizon should notice nothing special."
finePrint: "In 1997 Hawking and Kip Thorne bet John Preskill that information would be lost. Hawking conceded in 2004, and Thorne refused to concede. Other proposals include soft hair (2016), fuzzballs, baby universes and objective collapse. Fuzzballs have been questioned for lacking an appropriate mechanism."
