title: "Ladder paradox"
titleAccent: "paradox"
chapter: time-physics
status: resolved
simplification: light
noMaths: true
order: 4
written: true
problem: "Can a ladder too long for a barn fit inside it, if it runs through fast enough?"
claimA: "Seen from the barn, the fast ladder is squashed shorter, so it fits inside."
claimB: "Seen from the ladder, the barn is squashed shorter, so the ladder fits even less."
verdict: "Whether both ends are inside at the same moment depends on who is watching."
whatBreaks: "The assumption that all observers agree which events happen at the same time."
statusNote: 'one claim is wrong. "At the same moment" has no single meaning for observers in relative motion.'
setup: "A ladder is 20 metres long when at rest. A barn is 12 metres long, with a door at each end. Someone runs the ladder through the barn at about 87 percent of the speed of light. Special relativity says moving objects are measured as shorter in the direction they move. For this speed, the factor is exactly two. From the barn, the ladder is 10 metres long, and it fits inside with room to spare. From the ladder, the barn is 6 metres long, and the ladder is far too big. Both cannot be true."
whyWrong: 'Both are true, but they are answers to different questions. "The ladder fits" really means "for a moment, both ends are inside the barn, and both doors are shut." That depends on two events, the doors shutting, being at the same time. Relativity says that observers in relative motion do not agree on that.'
resolution:
  - "In the barn's frame, both doors shut at the same instant with the whole ladder inside. In the ladder's frame, they do not shut together. The door at the far end shuts first, and it opens again before the front of the ladder reaches it. The door at the near end shuts later, after the back of the ladder has already gone past it. At no point is the whole ladder inside with both doors shut, so there is no contradiction."
  - "Each observer is correct about their own measurements. Nothing is wrong with the squashing. What had to be given up is the idea that two distant events are simultaneous for everyone."
finePrint: "If the far door stays shut and the ladder is stopped, the answer needs more care. No object is perfectly rigid in relativity, because a push cannot travel through it faster than light. The front of the ladder stops at the door, but the back keeps moving until the news reaches it, so the ladder is compressed or breaks. Wolfgang Rindler introduced this puzzle in 1961 in a version with a fast-walking man and a grate."
