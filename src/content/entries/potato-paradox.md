problem: "Potatoes that are 99% water dry out until they are 98% water. Do they lose much weight?"
claimA: "Going from 99% water to 98% water is a tiny change, so the potatoes lose very little weight."
claimB: "The potatoes end up weighing half as much as before."
verdict: "The dry part is what stays fixed, and it doubles as a share of the total."
whatBreaks: "The idea that a one-point change in the water percentage means a small change in weight."
statusNote: "the first claim is wrong. The percentage of dry matter doubles from 1% to 2%, so the total weight must halve."
setup: "Fred brings home 100 kg of potatoes. They are 99% water, and the rest is dry matter. He leaves them outside overnight, and they dry until they are 98% water. How much do they weigh now? The Universal Book of Mathematics is quoted by Wikipedia as stating the problem this way. Most people guess something close to 100 kg, perhaps 99 kg. The answer is 50 kg."
whyWrong: "It is natural to look at the water percentage, which barely moves, and assume the weight barely moves. But the water is not the part that stays fixed. The dry matter is. Only water evaporates, so the dry matter stays at exactly the same weight, and it is the share of dry matter that tells the story. It goes from 1% to 2%, which is double."
resolution:
  - "At the start, 1% of 100 kg is dry matter, which is 1 kg. That 1 kg does not change, because only the water leaves. At the end, the potatoes are 98% water, so the dry matter is 2% of the total. If 1 kg is 2% of the total, the total is 50 kg."
  - "Put another way, the dry matter has to become twice as large a share of the potatoes. It cannot grow, so the whole potato has to shrink to half its weight. Half of it was water that left."
  - "Wikipedia notes that, in Quine’s classification of paradoxes, this is a veridical paradox: one whose result is true even though it seems wrong."
finePrint: "This entry is about the arithmetic only. The potatoes are idealised, with nothing in them but water and dry matter, and nothing leaves except water. Real potatoes are not this tidy. The puzzle was a Puzzler on the Car Talk radio show, according to Wikipedia."
