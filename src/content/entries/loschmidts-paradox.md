problem: "If the laws of motion work the same forwards and backwards, why does time seem to run in only one direction?"
claimA: "If the laws of motion work the same forwards and backwards, then for every process in which entropy goes up there is an equally lawful process in which it goes down."
claimB: "In practice entropy goes up and never down: heat flows from hot to cold, and a broken cup does not mend itself."
verdict: "The time-symmetric laws alone do not explain why entropy rises toward the future. Proposed explanations appeal to probability, a very special beginning, or other assumptions."
whatBreaks: "The contested choice is between treating the second law as only very likely, and explaining the one-way direction through the special state the universe began in."
statusNote: "experts agree this is a real puzzle and disagree on which claim to give up. Each of the main approaches settles a part of it, and none is accepted as covering the whole."
setup: "In 1876 the physicist Josef Loschmidt objected to Ludwig Boltzmann’s derivation of the second law of thermodynamics. Boltzmann had shown that the entropy of a gas, a measure of disorder, rises over time. Loschmidt pointed out that if the laws of motion work equally well forwards and backwards, then reversing the velocity of every molecule at some moment gives a lawful history in which entropy falls. William Thomson, later Lord Kelvin, had defended the second law against a similar objection in 1874. The general form of the objection is that an irreversible process cannot be deduced from reversible dynamics."
whyWrong: "The puzzle comes from a clash between what the laws allow and what is seen. Every step of the reversed motion obeys the same laws as the forward one. Yet nobody has seen a gas gather itself back into one corner. Boltzmann’s calculation seems to have slipped in an extra assumption about the molecules, called molecular chaos, that does not follow from the laws of motion."
resolution:
  - "Several approaches are in play. They address different parts of the problem, and none is accepted as complete."
positions:
  - title: "The reversal is not realistic"
    body: "Binder (2023) argues that reversing every velocity needs measurements of the gas. Irreversible measurements raise entropy in the measuring device, and reversible ones fall under Landauer’s principle. Cost: this answers only the version about reversing one gas’s motion, and not the broader claim that an irreversible process cannot be deduced from reversible dynamics."
  - title: "The past was special"
    body: "The one-way direction comes from a very low-entropy start, the Big Bang, and not from the laws. Cost: the low-entropy start is itself left unexplained, and cosmic inflation is only an attempt to give a reason for it."
  - title: "Look at the dynamics"
    body: "Special mathematical methods for dynamical systems, such as transfer operators (Driebe, 1999), can show irreversibility emerging from the equations. Cost: the approach works well for only a handful of exactly solvable models."
finePrint: "Denis Evans and Debra Searles derived a fluctuation theorem that gives the odds of entropy running backwards in small systems. Thomas Gold once proposed a model in which the arrow of time reverses if the universe contracts. Both are mentioned without detail in the main source."
