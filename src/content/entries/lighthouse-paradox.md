problem: "Can the bright spot from a turning lighthouse cross a distant wall faster than light?"
claimA: "No bright spot produced by a lighthouse can appear to move across a wall faster than light."
claimB: "A sweeping spot can cross a distant wall faster than light, with nothing travelling along the wall that fast."
whatBreaks: "The assumption that a moving pattern must consist of one thing carrying information along its path."
statusNote: "a sweeping spot can move faster than light as a pattern; the light itself travels at light speed."
setup: "Picture a lighthouse beam turning across an enormous distant wall. A small change in the beam’s angle moves its point of contact a very long way along the wall."
whyWrong: "When the wall is distant enough, that spot’s sideways speed can exceed the speed of light. That seems to break relativity’s limit."
resolution:
  - "Each part of the wall is struck by different light sent from the lighthouse. Individual photons travel outward at light speed, not sideways along the wall."
  - "No message travels from the first bright patch to the next faster than light. A changing pattern can outrun light without transmitting a usable signal that way."
finePrint: "This is an ideal geometry example. A real beam spreads and takes time to reach the wall; none of that turns the apparent sideways spot into a physical object travelling along it."
