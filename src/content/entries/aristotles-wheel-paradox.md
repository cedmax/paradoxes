problem: "How do two fixed-together wheels of different sizes travel the same distance in one turn?"
claimA: "The small and large wheels both roll without slipping across the same distance."
claimB: "Wheels of different sizes cannot each roll without slipping the same distance in one turn."
whatBreaks: "The assumption that a circle carried by a rolling wheel must itself roll without slipping."
statusNote: "the smaller circle does not meet the ground as a rolling wheel, so its own circumference need not equal its distance travelled."
setup: "Draw a small circle inside a large circle and fix them together. Roll the outer circle one complete turn. Both centres move forward the same distance, and both circles turn once."
whyWrong: "The outer circle’s distance is one large circumference. The small one has a shorter circumference but has moved just as far. It seems as though one turn could equal two different lengths."
resolution:
  - "The outer rim rolls without sliding at the point where it touches the floor. The inner rim has no such contact and slips relative to an imaginary track beneath it."
  - "Both circles share the same forward motion, but that does not make each circumference equal the distance travelled. The two circles are carried along together."
finePrint: "The classic puzzle appears in a work traditionally linked to Aristotle but not securely written by him. The connection with [Galileo’s paradox](galileos-paradox) is a shared trap about comparing sizes, not an identical mathematical mechanism."
