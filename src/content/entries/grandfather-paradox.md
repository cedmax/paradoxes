title: "Grandfather paradox"
titleAccent: "paradox"
chapter: time-causation
status: disputed
simplification: light
noMaths: true
order: 10
written: true
sourcesVerified: true
problem: "If you travelled back in time and stopped your grandfather having children, how could you ever have been born?"
claimA: "A time traveller is free to do anything any other person could do, including stopping their own grandfather becoming a father."
claimB: "The past is fixed: you can only be there if you were born, so you cannot stop your own birth."
verdict: "Changing the past seems to undo the traveller’s own existence. Proposed answers keep one consistent history, introduce another history or rule out the travel, and none is established."
whatBreaks: "The idea that a person can both visit the past and be free to change it."
statusNote: "the puzzle is real, and there are several rival ways out. None can be tested, because no time machine is known to be possible."
setup: "Suppose a time machine exists. You travel back to before your parent was born, find your grandfather as a young man, and set out to kill him. If you succeed, your parent is never born, so you are never born, so you never travel back, so you never kill him. The story undoes itself."
whyWrong: "The trouble is two ideas that each sound sensible. One says you are free to act in the past like anyone else. The other says that what happened, happened, and you are part of it. Put together, they say you both can and cannot do it. Philosophers treat this as a test of what it would even mean for time travel to be possible."
resolution:
  - "A single consistent history lets the traveller take part in the past but not change what happened. A branching account sends the traveller into another history instead. A third response rejects travel to the past."
  - "These give different meanings to the traveller’s apparent freedom. The scenario does not establish which, if any, describes possible time travel."
positions:
  - title: "Only consistent histories"
    body: "Whatever you do in the past is part of what already happened, so something always stops you. Cost: it can feel as if your free choice is overruled, and it only explains why the paradox does not happen, not what stops you."
  - title: "Branching worlds"
    body: "You land in a different version of history, so your own past is untouched. Cost: it needs many real universes, and some physicists, such as Stephen Hawking, argued a traveller should still find a single consistent history in the world they left."
  - title: "Time travel is impossible"
    body: "The paradox is a sign that travel to the past cannot happen. Cost: general relativity has solutions that allow it on paper, so ruling it out needs another reason, which has not been agreed."
finePrint: "No time machine is known to be possible. Some solutions of Einstein's general relativity allow paths that loop back in time, but whether they could exist in our universe is unknown. All the answers above are proposals, not results. The paradox has been discussed in science fiction since at least the 1920s, long before anyone tried to fit it into physics."
