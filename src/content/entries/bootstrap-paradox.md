---
title: "Bootstrap paradox"
titleAccent: "paradox"
chapter: time-physics
status: disputed
simplification: light
noMaths: true
order: 2
written: true
problem: "If a time traveller hands you a watch that you later carry back to give to your younger self, where did the watch come from?"
claimA: "Everything that exists has to come from somewhere."
claimB: "A time loop could contain an object or a piece of information that has no origin."
verdict: "It breaks no logic, but philosophers disagree on whether such a loop is acceptable."
whatBreaks: "The idea that everything must have a cause or a beginning."
statusNote: "the puzzle is real. Experts disagree on whether to accept loops or reject them, and no time machine is known to be possible."
setup: "An old woman gives a young playwright a watch. Years later he travels back in time and gives the same watch to the same woman when she was young. She keeps it for life and hands it to him as an old woman. Nobody ever made the watch. It simply goes round the loop. The same can be told with information, such as a theory that a time traveller learns from a book and then passes on to its author."
whyWrong: "Nothing in the story contradicts itself, which makes it different from the grandfather paradox. The watch is not changed and nothing is undone. What bothers people is that the object seems to cause itself. We expect any chain of causes to start somewhere, and here it does not. The name comes from the saying about pulling yourself up by your own bootstraps."
resolution:
  - "Some philosophers say a loop like this is possible, and merely strange. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy reports that some defenders compare a loop to the Big Bang, which we also struggle to explain without calling it impossible. On this view, a loop is a brute fact with no further explanation, and that is allowed."
  - "Others say the loop is a real problem, because it contains something that happens with no explanation at all. The physicist Sergey Krasnikov argued that such loops would mean a physical system changing in a way its laws do not govern. Other physicists have worked on whether the usual rules about disorder in physical systems could still hold in a loop. That work is theoretical."
positions:
  - title: "Loops are possible"
    body: "A loop with no start is odd but not contradictory, like a universe with no explanation for its beginning. Cost: it accepts events that have no explanation."
  - title: "Loops are not acceptable"
    body: "An object or idea with no origin is a sign that something is wrong, so time travel to the past should be ruled out. Cost: it needs a reason to rule out time travel that does not just assume the answer."
finePrint: 'No time machine is known to be possible, and the answers above are proposals, not results. Writers use many names for this idea: causal loop, information loop and ontological paradox. Heinlein''s 1958 story "All You Zombies" is a well-known example, in which one character becomes their own mother and father.'
