problem: "An endless line of reapers, each killing a man only if no earlier reaper has, cannot exist. What does that show?"
claimA: "Something can have infinitely many causes, with no first cause among them."
claimB: "If endless chains of causes are possible, individually possible reapers can be combined into an endless line."
whatBreaks: "Whether to rule out endless chains of causes, or reject the step from individually possible reapers to this impossible arrangement."
statusNote: "the line is agreed to be impossible. Pruss and Koons conclude that endless chains of causes cannot exist, while Schmid and Malpass say parts that could each exist need not all exist together."
setup: "A man is alive at noon. One reaper will check him at 1pm, another at 12:30pm, another at 12:15pm, and so on, always halving the wait. There is no earliest reaper after noon. Each kills him if he is still alive and does nothing otherwise."
whyWrong: "He cannot survive, because a reaper would find him alive and kill him. But no reaper can kill him: there is always an earlier one that would already have done so. The conditions contradict each other."
resolution:
  - "The arrangement cannot exist. What is disputed is the lesson to draw from that fact. Is infinity in a chain of causes the problem, or is this simply an impossible combination of otherwise possible parts?"
positions:
  - title: "Limit chains of causes"
    body: "Pruss and Koons argue that, if the individual reapers are possible and possible pieces can be combined, the endless chain must be what fails. Cost: this places a substantial restriction on possible causes, and critics argue that the story does not justify it."
  - title: "Reject this combination"
    body: "Schmid and Malpass argue that a line with no first member cannot also make each member act exactly when no earlier one has acted. Possible pieces need not form a possible whole. Cost: critics say this gives up a plausible principle about combining possibilities and ask what prevents the combination."
finePrint: "The reapers are numbered in reverse time order: the reaper called “first” in some tellings acts last. Shackel analysed the incompatible conditions in 2005. His diagnosis can be accepted even by someone who also limits causal chains; the camps disagree over whether that further limit is needed. Such a limit need not forbid a past with no beginning, provided no individual chain of causes extends endlessly. Related puzzles use barriers at ever smaller distances. Unlike [Thomson’s lamp](thomsons-lamp), whose final state is not specified, this story’s conditions contradict one another."
