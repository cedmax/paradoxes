problem: "Can a ball that is perfectly still begin to move at an arbitrary time under Newton’s laws?"
claimA: "Newton’s laws always determine one future motion from a ball’s initial position and speed."
claimB: "On Norton’s specially shaped dome, the same resting start permits both staying still and beginning to move later."
verdict: "Newton’s force equation alone does not always guarantee a unique motion for an idealised system."
whatBreaks: "The assumption that giving the starting conditions and a force law always gives a single possible future."
statusNote: "the ideal dome uses a force law for which uniqueness fails; ordinary smooth force laws normally avoid this example."
setup: "A point-sized ball rests at the summit of a precisely shaped, frictionless dome. The usual motion equations allow it to stay put indefinitely."
whyWrong: "Surprisingly, the same equations also allow the ball to begin sliding at any later time without any added push. Both histories obey the equation."
resolution:
  - "The mathematical force close to the summit changes in a way that defeats the usual guarantee of one unique solution to a motion equation."
  - "The contradiction is with an assumed general promise of determinism, not with the algebra. Real domes, finite balls and disturbances do not behave like this perfect model."
finePrint: "The construction deliberately violates a mathematical condition often imposed for unique solutions. Whether it threatens physical determinism outside the ideal example remains contested."
