problem: "What happens when an unstoppable force meets an immovable object?"
claimA: "An unstoppable force and an immovable object can both exist in the same world."
claimB: "If the force truly cannot be stopped, then no object can be immovable against it."
whatBreaks: "The premise that both mutually exclusive absolutes can exist together."
statusNote: "with the everyday meanings, the imagined pair cannot both satisfy their definitions, so there is no collision to predict."
setup: "Picture a force that nothing can stop and a wall that nothing can move. Imagine the force hitting the wall."
whyWrong: "The force cannot be stopped, but the wall cannot be moved. Every imagined outcome seems to break one description."
resolution:
  - "At least one label must fail. In a world with a truly unstoppable force, the wall cannot remain absolutely immovable against it."
  - "This is a word-level impossibility, like the [omnipotence paradox](omnipotence-paradox), not a physics experiment involving very strong forces and sturdy walls."
finePrint: "The puzzle is related to the [omnipotence paradox](omnipotence-paradox) because both ask what absolute powers or properties mean. They are not identical puzzles: one involves incompatible descriptions of two things, the other a task assigned to an all-powerful being. Shackel (2018) builds an idealised model in which such a pair exists and meets, and both are destroyed. It changes what the absolutes mean. With the everyday meanings the clash remains."
