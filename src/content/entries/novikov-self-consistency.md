problem: "Could someone visit the past if anything they do there must already fit what happened?"
claimA: "Someone who can reach the past must also be able to change an event that happened there."
claimB: "In a single self-consistent history, everything the traveller does in the past must already be part of it."
verdict: "The self-consistency rule avoids contradictions by forbidding changes to an already established past."
whatBreaks: "Whether travel to the past would permit changes, require a single consistent history, or be impossible."
statusNote: "the self-consistency principle is a proposed constraint on time-travel histories, not an experimentally established law."
setup: "Imagine taking a time machine back to stop an event that is already recorded as having happened. The traveller could try, but the attempt might itself have always been part of the record."
whyWrong: "A trip to the past seems to give the traveller another chance to change things. But if there is just one history, changing an event can erase the cause of the trip."
resolution:
  - "Novikov’s proposal allows only histories in which every action, including the trip, fits the same past. The traveller might fail or unknowingly cause what they hoped to stop."
  - "This is a more specific proposed rule than the [grandfather paradox](grandfather-paradox). It prevents the contradiction but does not establish that travel to the past is physically possible."

    body: "Only self-consistent trips can happen. Cost: many apparently possible choices in the past become impossible to complete."
  - title: "No such trips"
    body: "Some other feature of physics rules out travel into one’s own past. Cost: a general prohibition has not been established for every theoretical spacetime model."
finePrint: "The principle concerns hypothetical paths through spacetime that return to the past. It should not be presented as a discovery that real time machines exist."
