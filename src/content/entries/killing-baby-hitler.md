problem: "If a time traveller prevented a particular person’s rise to power, what would their reason for going back become?"
claimA: "A traveller can go back and remove the person, so the later events never happen."

setup: "Wikipedia describes killing baby Hitler as a thought experiment about using backward time travel to kill the infant Adolf Hitler, and as a trope of science fiction since the Second World War. It says the question holds both an ethical dilemma and a version of the [grandfather paradox](grandfather-paradox), sometimes called the “Hitler’s murder paradox”. This entry covers only the timing puzzle. It does not take up the ethics of the act, which Wikipedia treats separately, and it takes no side on that question."
whyWrong: "The structure is the same as in the grandfather paradox. A traveller goes back to remove someone before the later events happen. But if the later events never happen, there is nothing for the traveller to prevent, and so no reason for the trip."
resolution:
  - "Wikipedia notes one plain inference. The person in the example died in 1945, so no time traveller can have killed them as an infant."
positions:
  - title: There is only one fixed timeline (B-theory of time)
    body: "On the B-theory of time, a trip back with the intention of removing the person would remove the traveller’s own reason for travelling. It is often concluded that, since the past has already happened, altering it is a logical impossibility. Cost: on this view, altering the past is a logical impossibility, so no trip can change what happened."
  - title: Time has two dimensions
    body: "Models that treat time as two-dimensional, with ordinary time and a second “hyper-time”, allow the past to be changed without contradiction. Wikipedia names hyper-eternalism, two-dimensional presentism and hyper-presentism. In these models both the past and the future can be changed. Cost: Wikipedia says it can be debated whether such models really change the past, or only cause a variation in hyper-time."
  - title: The trip creates a parallel universe
    body: "If time travel creates a parallel universe, the traveller only creates a new universe without the person in it, and the original universe goes on. Cost: Wikipedia reports that, on this view, the traveller’s act would not undo anything that happened in the original timeline. Astrophysicist Brian Koberlein concluded it would be inconsequential at best."
