problem: "Can a voting rule respect everyone’s private choices and always follow unanimous preferences?"
claimA: "A social rule can always respect minimal personal rights and every unanimous preference at once."
claimB: "Some sets of personal preferences make those two requirements incompatible."
verdict: "Sen’s result shows a clash between precise rules for personal choice and collective agreement."
whatBreaks: "Which attractive demand to restrict: individuals’ decisive say over a personal matter, or the rule that unanimous preferences must win."
statusNote: "the formal impossibility is established under its assumptions, but philosophers disagree about which assumption to give up."
setup: "Imagine two people with opposing tastes about what each person reads. Each has a recognised say over a choice considered their own. The same people also have preferences about what the other chooses."
whyWrong: "Following each person’s private right can create a collective outcome that both rank below another. But following that unanimous preference can override one person’s assigned right."
resolution:
  - "Amartya Sen showed that a modest formal rule of personal liberty can conflict with a rule requiring unanimous preferences to be respected."
  - "The theorem does not say private choice is bad or that unanimity is bad. It says these precise demands cannot be satisfied together for every possible set of preferences."
positions:
  - title: "Keep the individual rights rule"
    body: "Let personal decisions stand even when everyone ranks a different complete outcome higher. Cost: the rule can violate unanimity over social outcomes."
  - title: "Restrict the rights rule"
    body: "Do not give unconditional decisiveness over a private matter when preferences interact. Cost: personal liberty becomes conditional on others’ preferences."
finePrint: "The result depends on what a right means in the model: control over particular social comparisons. It is a social-choice impossibility result, not a general rejection of liberalism."
