problem: "Can reason establish both that the world began and that it never began?"
claimA: "The world must have a first moment, since an endless past could never be completed."
claimB: "The world cannot have a first moment, since there would be no earlier time in which its beginning could occur."
verdict: "Kant treated the conflict as a warning against applying ordinary ideas of time to the world as a whole."
whatBreaks: "Whether reasoning about time and causes can legitimately treat the whole world like one object within experience."
statusNote: "Kant offered a particular diagnosis of the opposing arguments; its wider account of space, time and knowledge remains debated."
setup: "Kant set out pairs of arguments that seem to reach opposite answers about the world as a whole. One pair asks whether time has a beginning. Another asks whether everything can be divided without end."
whyWrong: "Reasoning that works on events inside the world appears to trap itself when applied to the entire world and all of time."
resolution:
  - "Kant’s answer distinguishes the world as experienced from a supposed world as it exists independently of experience. That distinction is meant to block both overreaching proofs."
  - "The puzzle does not establish both claims as truths. It challenges the assumptions behind applying each proof to the whole of reality."
