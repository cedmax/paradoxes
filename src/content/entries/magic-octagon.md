why: "The tempting up-left guess treats the turn as clockwise from both sides. But a clockwise turn seen from the front appears anticlockwise from behind, so the hidden arrow moves from left towards down-left, not up-left. The flip changes the viewpoint, not the right angle between the two painted arrows."

Shire Post Mint describes a three-move demonstration with two vertical flips and one 45° turn; this interactive version isolates a single flip. Its history mentions Elias et al.’s Screwy Square (1946), Willane’s The Chinese Compass (1947), and Gardner’s Crazy Crackers (1949), credited to Val Evans. The modern copper coin was designed with Raymond Hall. The early history remains uncertain."
