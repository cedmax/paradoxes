description: "Turn an octagonal coin 45°, then flip it. Where will the arrow point?"
problem: "The front arrow points up; the flipped view shows an arrow pointing left. Turn the coin 45°, then flip it. Where will that arrow point?"
simplification: light
noMaths: false
order: 370
why: "The tempting up-left guess treats the turn as clockwise from both sides. But a clockwise turn seen from the front appears anticlockwise from behind, so the hidden arrow moves from left towards down-left, not up-left. The flip changes the viewpoint, not the right angle between the two painted arrows."
finePrint: "The outcome depends on the flip axis, which stays vertical in the picture even after the coin turns. Shire Post Mint describes a three-move demonstration with two vertical flips and one 45° turn; this interactive version isolates a single flip. Its history mentions Elias et al.’s Screwy Square (1946), Willane’s The Chinese Compass (1947), and Gardner’s Crazy Crackers (1949), credited to Val Evans. The modern copper coin was designed with Raymond Hall. Judge Gary R. Brown discusses a related effect in The Captain’s Nightmare (2020), but the earliest origin remains uncertain."
