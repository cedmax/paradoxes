problem: "Can hot water freeze sooner than cooler water put in the same freezer?"
claimA: "Under the same conditions, water that starts out hotter does not freeze first in any meaningful, reproducible way."
claimB: "Under some conditions, water that starts out hotter really does freeze before otherwise identical water that starts out cooler."
verdict: "Scientists still disagree on whether hot water really freezes first in a reproducible way, and what would cause it. A related speed-up in cooling has been shown in other systems."
whatBreaks: "The contested choice is between accepting that hot water can genuinely freeze first under some conditions, and treating the reported cases in water as errors of measurement or chance."
statusNote: "water experiments disagree, and their definitions of freezing differ. Broader cooling effects in other systems do not resolve that disagreement."
setup: "Put otherwise identical samples of hot and cooler water in a freezer. The hot water has further to cool, so it seems it must freeze later. Some experiments report the opposite. The question is whether the reversal is reproducible, and under which conditions."
whyWrong: "Starting temperature can change more than the distance to freezing. Hot water may evaporate more or melt insulating frost under its container. Water can also stay liquid below its usual freezing temperature before ice forms, an effect called supercooling. These differences can change the result, but they also make fair comparisons difficult."
resolution:
  - "Experiments on water disagree. Even “freezes first” needs a definition: reaching 0 °C, forming the first ice or becoming solid are different events."
  - "A broader effect, where a hotter start approaches a cold final state faster, has been demonstrated in other systems. That does not settle the everyday water question."
positions:
  - title: "Hot water sometimes freezes first"
    body: "Reports link reversals to evaporation, frost or different amounts of supercooling. Cost: the result depends on details and can be difficult to reproduce."
  - title: "A meaningful reversal is not established"
    body: "Controlled experiments have found no reversal and have identified measurement errors that could create one. Cost: some measured cooling to 0 °C rather than ice formation, leaving other versions of the claim open."
finePrint: "Erasto Mpemba reported the effect while making ice cream as a pupil, and he and Denis Osborne published experiments in 1969. The idea has older precedents. Burridge and Linden’s 2016 experiment measured cooling to 0 °C, and Katz objected that this is not the same as freezing. Later reviews retain the controversy in water while discussing well-established effects in other systems. A 2026 water experiment reported reversals associated with air currents and supercooling. These findings do not imply that hot water always freezes first or supply one universal explanation."
