---
title: Sorites paradox
titleAccent: paradox
chapter: vague-words
status: disputed
simplification: light
order: 1
written: true
problem: If you remove grains from a heap of sand one at a time, when does it stop being a heap?
claimA: One grain never turns a heap into a non-heap.
claimB: Take enough grains and the heap is gone.
verdict: Philosophers disagree on where the argument breaks.
whatBreaks: Words with no sharp edge, pushed one tiny step at a time.
statusNote: "most theorists of vagueness think the argument has a fault that can be found, but they disagree on where it is: a sharp hidden edge, truth by degrees, or a meaning that shifts with context."
setup: A million grains of sand piled up are a heap. One grain on its own is not. Take a single grain away from a heap and it is still a heap, because nobody can see the difference. But keep doing that, and the heap shrinks to one grain, which is not a heap. Somewhere a heap became not a heap. Where?
whyWrong: Words like "heap", "tall", "bald" and "rich" have no sharp edge. They work fine for clear cases and fall apart in between. The paradox takes that blur and chains it step by step, using the rule that a tiny change can never matter, until the blur swallows the whole pile.
resolution:
  - "Most specialists accept that a million grains make a heap and one grain does not, and look for the faulty step in between. Not everyone does. A few, called nihilists, such as Peter Unger and Samuel Wheeler, conclude that words like “heap” apply to nothing, so there were never any heaps to begin with. Others deny that the argument is valid at all."
  - "The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy notes that most theorists of vagueness suppose the paradox is solvable: the argument is defective somewhere. That is why the entry is marked Disputed rather than Unreconciled. The rival diagnoses, such as a sharp but unknowable edge, truth by degrees, and meaning that shifts with context, each claim to have found the faulty step, and the disagreement is over which one is right."
positions:
  - title: There is a sharp edge
    body: "A real cut-off exists, but nobody can know where it is. Cost: it feels absurd that one grain matters."
  - title: Heap fades gradually
    body: "Being a heap is a matter of degree, and each grain changes it by a sliver. Cost: it needs a more complicated logic than true or false."
  - title: Depends on the moment
    body: "What counts as a heap shifts with the situation and the person asking. Cost: it dodges the question of where any single edge sits."
finePrint: These are three of several proposed answers, shown in plain terms. None of them is accepted by most specialists, which is why this entry is marked Disputed.
sources:
