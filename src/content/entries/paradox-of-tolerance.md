title: Paradox of tolerance
format: argument
chapter: collective
status: disputed
simplification: light
noMaths: true
order: 50
sourcesVerified: true
problem: Should a tolerant society tolerate people who want to end tolerance?
claimA: "The intolerant should be tolerated unless they pose a real danger or incite violence."
claimB: "A tolerant society may claim the right to suppress movements that preach intolerance, by force if necessary."
verdict: "Protecting tolerance may require limits, but philosophers disagree about when restricting an intolerant movement is justified."
whatBreaks: "Whether tolerance should stop only at real danger and incitement, or earlier, at movements that preach intolerance. Experts split on which."
statusNote: "the dispute concerns the threshold for restriction. The proposed limits differ, and deciding what counts as intolerant or dangerous can itself be abused."
setup: "A society protects people’s freedom to hold and express views they oppose. Some movements use that freedom to argue that others should lose it. Allowing them to grow may threaten tolerance. Restricting them seems to deny the freedom the society protects."
whyWrong: "Both aims matter: giving room to disagreement and preserving the conditions that make disagreement possible. Neither “tolerate everyone” nor “suppress the intolerant” settles where to draw a justified limit."
resolution:
  - "Popper argued that unlimited tolerance could destroy tolerance. He also opposed suppressing intolerant views while argument and public opinion could keep them in check. His note claimed a right to restrict movements preaching intolerance."
  - "Rawls required a reasonable danger to security or the institutions of liberty. A narrower free-speech approach restricts such speech only in limited cases. These approaches do not draw the boundary in the same place."
positions:
  - title: "Restrict movements preaching intolerance"
    body: "Popper’s note claims this right while preferring argument when it works. Cost: deciding what counts as intolerance gives authorities power that can silence legitimate dissent."
  - title: "Require a threat to liberty"
    body: "Rawls says a just society should tolerate the intolerant unless security or the institutions of liberty are reasonably in danger. Cost: movements can grow until that point, and the judgement of danger remains contested."
  - title: "Protect speech with narrow exceptions"
    body: "Keep speech free except in limited cases such as incitement to imminent lawless action. Cost: movements can undermine tolerance without crossing that line."
finePrint: "The claims summarise competing thresholds, not two quotations. Popper’s discussion is an endnote in The Open Society and Its Enemies (1945). It does not call for suppressing every intolerant opinion. Rawls discusses the issue in A Theory of Justice (1971). Legal rules differ between countries and do not by themselves settle the philosophical question. The label “intolerant” is itself disputed. No judgement about any current movement is made here."
