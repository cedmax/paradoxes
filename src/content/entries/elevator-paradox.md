problem: "Why does the first lift to arrive so often seem to be going the wrong way?"
claimA: "If lifts go up and down, arriving at a random moment should find equal numbers going each way."
claimB: "At some floors, the lift spends much more time travelling in one direction before passing, so a random arrival sees that direction more often."
verdict: "The direction observed depends on how long each part of the lift’s route lasts and where the observer waits."
whatBreaks: "The assumption that observing at a random time samples up and down trips equally."
statusNote: "at a floor near one end of a route, the timing of stops and journeys can bias which direction is first seen."
setup: "An office worker on a low floor keeps seeing a lift arrive while going down, even though the same lift must also go up."
whyWrong: "There is no reason for the lift to make more downward journeys than upward ones overall, so each direction should seem equally common."
resolution:
  - "At a given floor, the wait to an approaching lift depends on its position in the whole route, not simply on counting up and down journeys."
  - "The [inspection paradox](inspection-paradox) likewise shows how random moments sample durations rather than treating each interval equally."
finePrint: "The exact bias depends on the building, floor, lift scheduling and waiting rule. The usual story is an illustration of sampling and does not say all lift systems behave alike."
