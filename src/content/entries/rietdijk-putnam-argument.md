problem: "Does disagreement about what is happening now mean tomorrow already exists?"
claimA: "The future is not already real merely because distant observers disagree about what is present."
claimB: "If every observer’s whole present is equally real, differing presents seem to include events another observer calls future."
verdict: "Relativity establishes differing slices of the present; whether this makes the future already real is a philosophical argument."
whatBreaks: "Whether the different sets of events called present imply that all events across time exist equally."
statusNote: "the relativity of simultaneity is established, but the further conclusion that the future is fixed remains an interpretation."
setup: "Two observers moving relative to one another draw different lines across spacetime for events they call simultaneous. A faraway event on one person’s line of “now” may lie to the future of another person’s line."
whyWrong: "If each whole line of “now” is just as real, an event not yet present for one observer is already present for another. The argument uses that to support a world where past, present and future all exist."
resolution:
  - "The diagram shows two different simultaneity lines crossing the same encounter. It does not show that one observer sees the future happen earlier."
  - "Unlike the [ladder paradox](ladder-paradox), where the disagreement resolves a measurement puzzle, this argument adds a claim about which events are real."
positions:
  - title: "All events are equally real"
    body: "Treat relativity as support for a four-dimensional spacetime in which no observer’s present is privileged. Cost: everyday ideas of an open and moving present must be reinterpreted."
  - title: "Relativity need not settle existence"
    body: "Accept differing simultaneity lines without equating every observer’s present with what exists. Cost: this needs a separate account of what “exists now” means."
