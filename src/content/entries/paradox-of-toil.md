problem: "Can everyone becoming more willing to work make employment fall?"
claimA: "When more people are willing to work for lower pay, overall employment must rise."
claimB: "In certain slumps, greater willingness to work can lower wages and demand enough to reduce employment."
verdict: "In a model where interest rates cannot fall further, lower wages can deepen a slump instead of bringing more jobs."
whatBreaks: "Whether lower wages and greater labour supply necessarily create more jobs when spending is already weak."
statusNote: "the model produces the result under restrictive conditions, and economists dispute how much it explains in practice."
setup: "During a sharp slump, many people offer to work harder or accept lower wages. Businesses would usually be expected to hire more when labour gets cheaper."
whyWrong: "But lower earnings also mean less spending. If borrowing rates cannot fall enough to lift demand, firms may need fewer workers even though workers offer more labour."
resolution:
  - "This is a proposed labour-market counterpart of the [paradox of thrift](paradox-of-thrift): what helps one individual can weaken demand if everyone does it at the same time."
  - "The result is conditional on a model of a slump, not a claim that extra work normally destroys jobs."
