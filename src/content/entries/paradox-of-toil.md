problem: "Can everyone becoming more willing to work make employment fall?"
claimA: "When people offer more work during a severe slump, firms should employ more of them."
claimB: "When people offer more work during that slump, firms may employ fewer because wages and spending fall."
verdict: "Eggertsson’s slump model predicts that greater willingness to work can reduce jobs; whether this occurs depends on the economic conditions."
whatBreaks: "Whether lower labour costs mainly encourage hiring or deepen weak demand when borrowing rates cannot fall."
statusNote: "Eggertsson’s model predicts a reversal at very low interest rates; later models disagree on how robust the effect is."
setup: "In a serious slump, more people become willing to work for less pay. A business would normally be expected to hire when wages fall."
whyWrong: "If spending is already weak and borrowing rates cannot fall further, lower wages can lower prices, raise the real burden of debt and further depress demand. Firms may then employ fewer people despite cheaper labour."
resolution:
  - "Gauti Eggertsson described the paradox of toil in a model where nominal interest rates are stuck at zero and there is pressure for prices to fall. More willingness to work can deepen the slump."
  - "Like the [paradox of thrift](paradox-of-thrift), it reverses an intuitive individual-to-group prediction. But toil works through labour supply, wages and weak demand, so it is related rather than the same puzzle."
positions:
  - title: "The reversal matters"
    body: "Eggertsson’s model finds that greater labour supply at a zero interest rate can cut demand and employment. Cost: the result depends on the model’s crisis conditions."
  - title: "The reversal need not occur"
    body: "Other models find employment may rise as wage and household responses vary. Cost: they must account for the weak-demand channel identified in the original model."
finePrint: "Eggertsson’s 2010 paper concerns a zero nominal interest rate, falling prices and depressed output. A 2022 two-household model finds that the effect depends on the share of households constrained by current income. This is a theoretical result, not a universal observation."
