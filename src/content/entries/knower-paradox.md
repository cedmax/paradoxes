problem: "What happens to the sentence “This sentence is not known” if someone proves it?"
claimA: "Anything established by a valid proof can be known."
claimB: "The sentence “This sentence is not known” can be proved but cannot consistently be known."
verdict: "Applying ordinary rules about proof and knowledge to this sentence produces both knowledge and its denial."
whatBreaks: "Which unrestricted rule must change: that knowing makes something true, that proof gives knowledge, or that self-reference is allowed without limits."
statusNote: "the clash has several proposed diagnoses, and the unrestricted rules about self-referential knowledge cannot all survive."
setup: "Consider the sentence “This sentence is not known”. If someone knows it, then what it says is true, so it is not known. That rules out knowing it. But this very argument seems to prove its sentence."
whyWrong: "If proof always gives knowledge, the proved sentence is known after all. The result says it both is and is not known."
resolution:
  - "The paradox resembles the [Liar](liar-paradox), but speaks about knowledge rather than plain truth. It forces a choice about how self-reference, proofs and knowledge fit together."
positions:
  - title: "Restrict knowledge claims"
    body: "Do not allow the sentence to talk about its own knowledge in an unrestricted way. Cost: everyday-sounding statements need strict limits."
  - title: "Restrict proof to knowledge"
    body: "A proof of this self-referential sentence need not produce knowledge of it. Cost: an attractive link between proof and knowledge is weakened."
finePrint: "The problem is formalised using rules that knowledge implies truth and that proof yields knowledge. Philosophers disagree over which rule or logical principle needs qualification."
