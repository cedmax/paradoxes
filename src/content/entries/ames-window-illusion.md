problem: "Can a flat window turn continuously in one direction but seem to swing backwards?"
why: "The window is a flat trapezoid whose two vertical sides are different lengths. Its shape resembles a rectangular window seen at an angle. As the trapezoid rotates, the apparent depth of its tall and short sides can switch, making continuous turning look like repeated reversals. Some viewers see the real rotation instead."
finePrint: "Ames described the rotating trapezoid illusion in 1951. The effect depends on viewpoint and perspective cues; adding an unambiguous view of the window's orientation can reduce the apparent reversals. This page draws a flat rotating trapezoid by projecting its four corners from three-dimensional coordinates, rather than reproducing a photographed apparatus. The overlay gives a top-down orientation reference, not a measurement of anyone's perception."
