problem: "When a friend in a sealed lab sees one quantum measurement result, how can an observer outside describe the whole lab as a superposition?"
claimA: "Ordinary quantum rules completely describe the whole sealed lab, including the friend, as a superposition rather than one settled result."
claimB: "The friend’s measurement has produced one settled result, so the whole lab is no longer in that superposition."
whatBreaks: "How to combine quantum rules for the whole lab with the claim that the friend’s measurement has already ended its superposition."
statusNote: "the answers say a conscious mind ends the superposition, that the quantum rules are changed or supplemented, that every outcome happens in some branch, or that facts depend on who is looking."
setup: "A friend measures a particle inside a sealed lab and sees one result, either 0 or 1. An observer outside applies quantum theory to the whole lab. The theory gives a superposition: “the particle is 0 and the friend saw 0” together with “the particle is 1 and the friend saw 1”. A superposition is a quantum combination, not simply ignorance about a result already chosen."
whyWrong: "The friend reports one definite result. The outside quantum description keeps both possibilities together. Does the friend’s measurement end that superposition, and if so, how? The [Schrödinger’s cat](schrodingers-cat) puzzle now includes someone inside who can reason and report a result."
resolution:
  - "This is a thought experiment about applying quantum theory to observers themselves. It does not establish that a person literally experiences two results at once."
  - "Extended versions use several labs and observers to show that a particular set of assumptions about quantum rules, single outcomes and agreement between observers cannot all hold. They sharpen the problem without choosing its solution."
positions:
  - title: "Consciousness makes a result"
    body: "Wigner originally suggested that becoming conscious of a result ends the superposition. Cost: this gives minds a physical role outside the ordinary quantum laws, and Wigner later abandoned the idea."
  - title: "Change or supplement the theory"
    body: "Collapse theories change the rules to select an outcome; theories such as Bohmian mechanics add definite underlying properties. Cost: these alter or extend the theory and face their own problems, including compatibility with relativity."
  - title: "Every outcome happens"
    body: "Different branches contain friends who each see one result. Cost: explaining probability and evidence is difficult if every possible outcome occurs."
  - title: "Facts depend on perspective"
    body: "Different observers may correctly give different accounts. Cost: explaining how they establish shared facts and test a theory becomes harder."
finePrint: "The outside description assumes ordinary evolution without collapse and that it describes the lab completely. An ideal experiment would need extreme control over the lab; the issue is not merely that the observer has not asked a question. Interaction with the surroundings hides observable interference between the alternatives, a process called decoherence. That does not by itself choose an interpretation. Frauchiger and Renner’s extended argument uses specific assumptions about agents transferring each other’s certain conclusions, universal quantum theory and single outcomes. Its interpretation is debated. Relational quantum mechanics and QBism differ even though both allow observer-dependent descriptions. The positions here are broad summaries, not a claim that all versions of each interpretation face exactly the same objections."
