problem: "Which came first, the chicken or the egg?"
claimA: "Every chicken hatches from an egg laid by a chicken, so neither can come first."
claimB: "Chickens have not existed for ever, so one of them, or one of their eggs, did come first."
verdict: "Chickens evolved from animals that were not chickens, so the egg came first."
whatBreaks: "The assumption that every chicken must come from a chicken’s egg."
statusNote: "the first claim is wrong. Chickens had ancestors that were not chickens, so the first chicken hatched from an egg laid by something else."
setup: "The chicken or the egg causality dilemma is the question “which came first: the chicken or the egg?” It comes from the observation that all chickens hatch from eggs and all chicken eggs are laid by chickens. Wikipedia says it is also used as a metaphor for situations where it is unclear which of two events is the cause and which is the effect, or which of two jobs has to be done first. Plutarch posed it as a philosophical question in his essay “The Symposiacs” in the 1st century CE. Wikipedia reports that Aristotle concluded the sequence was infinite, with no true origin."
whyWrong: "The dilemma assumes that chickens have always existed, or that each one must come from another chicken’s egg. Wikipedia says evolutionary biology gives a literal answer, using the Darwinian idea that species change over time, so chickens had ancestors that were not chickens. It adds that the Greek philosopher Anaximander took a similar view when addressing the paradox."
resolution:
  - "If the question is about eggs in general, Wikipedia says the egg came first. The first amniote egg, a hard-shelled egg that could be laid on land, appeared about 312 million years ago. Chickens are domesticated descendants of the red junglefowl and probably arose little more than 8,000 years ago, at most."
  - "If the question is about chicken eggs, Wikipedia says the answer is still the egg, but the explanation is more complicated. The chicken arose through interbreeding and domestication of several wild junglefowl, a process that is poorly understood. The point where the evolving animal became a chicken is a somewhat arbitrary distinction, as with the [sorites paradox](sorites-paradox). Whatever criteria are chosen, an almost identical proto-chicken laid a fertilised egg that held DNA making it a chicken, because of mutations in the mother’s egg cell, the father’s sperm, or the fertilised cell."
  - "Wikipedia notes an argument about a protein, ovocleidin-17, that hens use to build a thickened eggshell. Because the hen makes it and not the egg, the first bird with it would have hatched from an unreinforced egg, which would put the chicken first. But the protein, or a similar one, is found in turkeys and finches, which suggests such proteins are common to all birds and are much older than the first chickens."
finePrint: "Wikipedia reports that, by the end of the 16th century, the question seemed settled in the Christian world on the basis of the Bible’s creation story, which allows for a first chicken that did not come from an egg, and that later Enlightenment philosophers began to question this. This entry covers only the biological answer and takes no position on any religious account."
