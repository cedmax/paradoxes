problem: "Which came first, the chicken or the egg?"
claimA: "Every chicken hatched from an egg laid by a chicken, so the chain of chickens and eggs has no first link."
claimB: "Chickens have not always existed, so the chain of chickens and eggs must have a first link."
verdict: "Chickens evolved from birds that were not chickens, so the chain does have a start, and eggs existed long before any chicken."
whatBreaks: "The rule that every chicken hatched from an egg laid by a chicken."
statusNote: "the rule that every chicken hatched from a chicken’s egg is false. The earliest chickens hatched from eggs laid by birds that were not quite chickens. Exactly where to draw the line between the two is vague, as in the [sorites paradox](sorites-paradox), but that does not change which claim fails."
setup: "All chickens hatch from eggs, and all chicken eggs are laid by chickens. So which came first? Plutarch posed the question as a philosophical problem in his Symposiacs, in the 1st century CE. Aristotle, four centuries earlier, had concluded that the sequence goes back for ever, with no true beginning. The phrase “chicken and egg” is now also used for any case where it is unclear which of two things causes the other, or which of two jobs has to be done first."
whyWrong: "Followed backwards, the rule that every chicken comes from a chicken’s egg never stops: behind each chicken there is an egg, and behind each egg another chicken. That reasoning is sound. If the rule held, chickens would have to go back for ever. They do not, so the rule must fail somewhere. The rule is believable because each step is tiny. A chick is almost exactly like its parents, so it seems no single generation could be the one where a non-chicken laid an egg that hatched into a chicken. That is the same pattern as the one-grain rule in the sorites paradox: a change too small to matter, repeated many times, adds up to a big one."
resolution:
  - "Evolution gives the literal answer. Species change slowly over many generations, so chickens had ancestors that were not chickens. Chickens are domesticated descendants of the red junglefowl, a wild bird, and probably arose no more than about 8,000 years ago, through a process of domestication and interbreeding that is still poorly understood. Eggs are far older. The amniotic egg, the kind that can be laid on land instead of in water, goes back at least about 312 million years, to the oldest known fossils of the animal group that includes reptiles, birds and mammals. So if the question is about eggs in general, the egg came first by hundreds of millions of years."
  - "If the question is about chicken eggs, the answer depends on what “chicken egg” means. If it means an egg with a chicken inside, the egg still came first. However the line between chicken and not-yet-chicken is drawn, at some point a bird that was almost a chicken laid an egg holding a chick whose genes, changed slightly by mutation, made it a chicken. If it means an egg laid by a chicken, then the first chicken came before the first such egg. Either way, the chain has a first link, and the rule that every chicken came from a chicken’s egg is false. The philosopher Ludger Jansen has argued that the precise answer depends on which biological theory of species is assumed, because species words like “chicken” are vague."
  - "In 2010 researchers at the universities of Sheffield and Warwick used computer simulations to show how a protein called ovocleidin-17, made by the hen, helps calcium carbonate crystals form in the eggshell. The universities’ press release said the work “may also give a partial answer” to the old question, and news reports announced that British scientists claimed to have solved the mystery, in favour of the chicken. One of the researchers, Mark Rodger, later asked whether the work really proved that the chicken came before the egg, and answered that it “further underlines that it’s a fun but pointless question”. The same protein, or one very like it, has since been found in turkeys and zebra finches, which suggests that shell-building proteins of this kind are common to birds and much older than chickens."
finePrint: "By the end of the 16th century, many in the Christian world took the question as settled by the creation story in the Bible, which allows for a first chicken that did not hatch from an egg. Later Enlightenment philosophers questioned that answer. This entry covers only the biological answer and takes no position on any religious account. The 8,000-year figure for chickens is an upper limit, not an exact date."
