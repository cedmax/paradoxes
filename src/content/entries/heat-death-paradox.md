problem: "If the universe had already existed forever, why had it not run out of usable energy?"
claimA: "An eternal, unchanging universe following ordinary irreversible processes should already have reached equilibrium."
claimB: "The observable universe still contains stars, temperature differences and processes far from equilibrium."
verdict: "The argument challenges an eternal, static picture of the universe rather than proving that every possible eternal universe is impossible."
whatBreaks: "The assumption that the universe is an eternal unchanging system to which a simple equilibration argument applies."
statusNote: "the classic tension depends on an older static-universe assumption; modern cosmology describes an expanding, evolving universe."
setup: "Heat tends to flow from warmer places to cooler places, and energy becomes less available to do work. Given unlimited past time in a fixed closed system, everything seems likely to have settled down."
whyWrong: "The universe is visibly not in that state. Stars shine, planets are warm or cold, and many changes are still happening."
resolution:
  - "The puzzle shows that an infinitely old, static universe with finite usable energy is hard to reconcile with irreversible processes."
  - "The modern universe is not well described by that simple model. Expansion, gravitation and cosmic history alter how entropy and equilibrium must be understood. Like [Olbers’ paradox](olbers-paradox), the argument tests an eternal static cosmos."
