problem: "Can what is happening right now prove that it is not a dream?"
claimA: "Knowing that someone is awake requires ruling out the possibility that they are dreaming."

verdict: "Dreams raise a serious doubt about what experience can prove; whether knowledge requires excluding dreams is disputed."
whatBreaks: "Whether knowing that one is awake requires excluding every possible dream that feels the same."
statusNote: "the dream challenge is widely discussed, but philosophers disagree about whether it defeats ordinary knowledge."
setup: "Descartes noted that people sometimes dream they are sitting awake, looking at things and making ordinary judgements. Dreams can feel convincing at the time."
whyWrong: "An experience offered as proof of being awake might itself occur inside a dream. But demanding proof against every possible dream may ask more of ordinary knowledge than it needs."
resolution:
  - "Descartes used the doubt to question what can be known from the senses alone. Sceptical arguments say that if a dream cannot be ruled out, experience cannot settle whether one is awake."
  - "Other responses allow knowledge without absolute certainty, or appeal to the wider coherence of waking memories and experiences. These checks can help in practice but can also be dreamed."
positions:
  - title: "Require proof against dreams"
    body: "The sceptical challenge associated with Descartes doubts whether sensory experience provides knowledge without ruling out dreaming. Cost: this threatens many ordinary claims to knowledge."
  - title: "Allow knowledge without certainty"
    body: "Fallibilist approaches permit knowing one is awake even if a dream remains logically possible. Cost: they must explain why the remaining possibility of error does not defeat knowledge."
finePrint: "Descartes raised the doubt in the First Meditation and later appealed to the coherence of waking life. His reply does not settle the wider philosophical question."
