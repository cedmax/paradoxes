problem: "Can what is happening right now prove that it is not a dream?"
claimA: "The sights and sounds of the present moment prove with certainty that the person experiencing them is awake."
claimB: "A dream can seem just like waking experience, so those sights and sounds alone cannot provide that proof."
verdict: "Experience alone cannot give a watertight test for wakefulness, though ordinary knowledge need not demand absolute certainty."
whatBreaks: "The assumption that a vivid experience, by itself, rules out an equally vivid dream."
statusNote: "the senses alone do not supply the absolute proof demanded by the question."
setup: "A dream can contain a desk, a conversation and the feeling of being completely awake. On waking, the whole scene turns out to have been a dream. What rules out the same mistake now?"
whyWrong: "Everything offered as evidence of being awake could itself appear inside a dream. The dreamer might even dream of checking the clock or pinching an arm."
resolution:
  - "The dream argument is a challenge to certainty, not evidence that the current experience really is a dream."
  - "It is possible to distinguish dreams from waking life using wider evidence, memory and reliable checks without claiming that a single moment of experience proves wakefulness beyond all doubt."
finePrint: "Descartes used dreaming to question what can be known from the senses. Different theories of knowledge demand different standards of certainty."
