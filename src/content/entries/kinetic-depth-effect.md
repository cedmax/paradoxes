problem: "Can dots on a flat screen look like a three-dimensional sphere only when they move?"
why: "A still projection of scattered dots can look flat. If the dots follow the paths produced by a rotating sphere, their different speeds and directions can make the sphere's depth become apparent. The screen remains flat, and a single static frame does not determine which dots are nearer."
finePrint: "Wallach and O'Connell studied the kinetic depth effect in 1953 by projecting shadows of rotating wire or solid forms onto a screen. Later work used unconnected dots on rotating surfaces. Mather (1989) found that timing between projected frames matters. This demonstration uses 108 fixed points distributed through a thick spherical shell, rather than just on its surface, with orthographic projection and without shading or perspective size changes. The perceived rotation may reverse because this projection does not say which half is nearer."
