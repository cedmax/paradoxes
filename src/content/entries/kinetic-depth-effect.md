problem: "Can dots on a flat screen look like a three-dimensional sphere only when they move?"
why: "A still projection of scattered dots can look flat. If the dots follow the paths produced by a rotating sphere, their different speeds and directions can make the sphere's depth become apparent. The screen remains flat, and a single static frame does not determine which dots are nearer."
finePrint: "Wallach and O'Connell described the kinetic depth effect in 1953 using rotating wire forms and their shadows. Later work used unconnected dots on rotating surfaces. Mather (1989) found that timing between projected frames matters. This demonstration uses a mathematical sphere of fixed points and orthographic projection, without shading, perspective size changes or occlusion."
