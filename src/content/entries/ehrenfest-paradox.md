problem: "Can a spinning disc keep the ordinary relation between its radius and its rim?"
claimA: "A spinning disc can remain perfectly rigid and keep ordinary flat geometry for all its co-rotating measurements."
claimB: "Special relativity makes rim and radial measurements behave differently, so that ordinary flat relation cannot hold for those measurements."
whatBreaks: "The assumption that a perfectly rigid rotating disc can be measured with everyday flat geometry."
statusNote: "perfect rigidity is incompatible with relativity, and distances measured around a rotating disc require care."
setup: "On a stationary disc, the circumference is about 6.28 times the radius. Now imagine spinning the disc very fast while insisting that it stays perfectly rigid."
whyWrong: "The rim moves around the axis while its radius points outward. Relativity affects measurements along motion, so rim and radial measurements do not fit the simple rigid-wheel picture."
resolution:
  - "A disc cannot be spun up as an absolutely rigid object; stresses and signals travel through matter at finite speeds."
  - "Observers rotating with the disc use a geometry in which the measured circumference-to-radius relation differs from the ordinary flat one. Like the [ladder paradox](ladder-paradox), this needs attention to which observer measures what."
finePrint: "In the stationary laboratory frame, the circular rim still has the usual coordinate circumference for its radius. The non-Euclidean relation concerns distances measured using rotating observers and their clocks, not a simple claim that the visible rim literally contracts to a smaller circle."
