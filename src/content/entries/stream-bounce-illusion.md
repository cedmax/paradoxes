problem: "When two dots cross, can one click change whether they seem to collide?"
why: "Two identical dots converge, overlap and separate. Without a sound, they can seem to pass through each other. A short click near the crossing makes some viewers see them bounce instead. The paths on screen are unchanged; sound changes how the ambiguous meeting is interpreted."
finePrint: "Sekuler, Sekuler and Lau tested the effect in 1997. They varied whether a short click came near the moment the targets coincided, and found more reports of bouncing. This demonstration uses its own disc sizes, timing and click sound, and is not a recording of their experiment. Audio latency, particularly over wireless speakers, can weaken the alignment."
